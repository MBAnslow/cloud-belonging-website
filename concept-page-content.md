## Page Header

**Title:** Belonging, interbeing and a cloud  
**Description:** How can art and technology can create sublime moments that stay with you? We explored an experience of nature a little like a hallucination, as we would see the world but with heightened sensory stimulation and a mystical narrative arc that eventuallys dissolves back into the everyday world.

## Part 1: Our slice of belonging

**Part 1 Lede:** What belonging means to us, how it reaches beyond people, and why we approached it through art.

### Section 1.1: What belonging means

- **01: A fundamental human need**  
  *Text:* Belonging is a universal, evolutionarily rooted drive to form and keep meaningful connections. Kelly-Ann Allen writes that our need to belong is like our need for water—in Maslow’s hierarchy, it comes right after food, water and safety. It is felt as being accepted, valued and understood.  
  *Cite:* Baumeister & Leary, 1995; Allen, 2020

- **02: A process, not a state**  
  *Text:* Belonging is never fixed. It flickers, depends on context, and is renegotiated all the time. It is shaped by autonomy, choice and commitment, so it can’t be imposed.  
  *Cite:* Allen, 2020

- **03: Built in small acts**  
  *Text:* Sharing a thought or a memory, listening, affirming, moving in time with someone. And reframing shared struggles as normal, so that difficulty doesn’t read as proof that you don’t fit in.  
  *Cite:* Walton & Cohen, 2011

- **04: Quality over quantity**  
  *Text:* What matters is frequent, warm, stable contact within real mutual care. Going from no close relationships to two changes everything; going from twenty to twenty-two changes very little.  
  *Cite:* Baumeister & Leary, 1995

- **05: Embodied and placed**  
  *Text:* Belonging lives in the body and attaches to places, objects, sounds, smells and textures. Think of a place where you belong and every sense comes back at once.  
  *Cite:* Allen, 2020

### Section 1.2: Belonging and nature

**Eyebrow:** Outside of social connection  
**Title:** Belonging and nature  

**Paragraph 1:**  
Socially speaking, belonging is the felt sense of being accepted, included, understood, welcomed, liked, and appreciated. But belonging extends beyond our relationships with others. It is also about how we relate to ourselves, how comfortable we feel in our own skin, and how connected we feel to the world around us.

**Paragraph 2:**  
Belonging is not only about being accepted by others, but also about feeling at home in ourselves and in the world. It provides a sense of safety and rootedness from which we can explore, engage with, and experience the world more openly. In this way, belonging is more than a social need; it is a foundation for curiosity, openness, exploration, and meaningful connection.

**Paragraph 3:**  
When your social sense of connection is damaged, often the only moments of solace come from our connection to the world. Nature provides a neutral and available point of reconnection.

### Section 1.3: Interbeing

**Eyebrow:** Interbeing  
**Title:** Interbeing: there is a cloud in your tea  

**Paragraph 1:**  
Thich Nhat Hanh, the late Vietnamese Zen master and peace activist, often pointed to a cup of tea and saw a cloud in it. The cloud has not died: it became rain, then a river, then this tea. Nothing is only itself. It *inter-is* with everything that made it possible.

**Paragraph 2:**  
It was from this concept of interbeing that we drew our core inspiration for Cloudfulness, choosing a breathing cloud as the physical medium of our installation. Where belonging is warm, social and fragile, interbeing is quiet and continuous. It never really goes away. Our project lives between the two—using the cloud to invite people to feel connected to each other, and also to something larger that was here before them and will continue after: weather, water, sky.

## Part 2: Installation design

**Part 2 Lede:** Designing a room with a cloud and a narrative experience for interbeing.

### Section 2.1: Building interbeing into a room

**Eyebrow:** Design features of belonging  
**Title:** Building interbeing into a room  

- **01: Embodied belonging**  
  *Description:* Belonging is experienced through the body and anchored in the senses. Removing your shoes and settling beneath the cloud creates a sense of physical grounding. The softness of the beanbag, the cloud’s texture, the warmth of its gentle illumination, and the sudden intensity of lightning engage the senses, drawing your attention into the present moment.  
  *Design Choice:* A multisensory space

- **02: Belonging is a journey**  
  *Description:* Dusk, a magical twilight, then breath synchrony: the experience moves, and you move with it.  
  *Design Choice:* An evolving score

- **03: Belonging is dynamic**  
  *Description:* A sensor follows your breathing, and the cloud’s light rises and settles with it. You breathe with the cloud rather than control it.  
  *Design Choice:* Biofeedback

- **04: Belonging depends on the quality of connections**  
  *Description:* One sky over everyone in the room, with nobody in a better seat.  
  *Design Choice:* A shared experience

- **05: We want you to carry this experience with you**  
  *Description:* The otherworldly aspects of the installation are sandwiched between everyday cloud states (dusk and daylight). The audio gradually introduces the magical twilight and gently fades away into daylight. These transitions were deliberately subtle to create continuity between experiences. The cloud you look up at might contain such a journey for you as well.  
  *Design Choice:* Subtle transitions back to everyday life

### Section 2.2: Why a cloud

**Eyebrow:** Why a cloud  
**Title:** Something we all live under  

1. **It is above everyone**  
   Nobody has a better seat. A cloud offers a shared perspective without a stage.
2. **It gives light a body**  
   A cloud is a volume for light to live in — glowing, flashing, catching the sun.
3. **It is always changing**  
   Clouds are impermanence made visible. They form, drift, rain and form again.
4. **It is water, and so are we**  
   The water cycle is continuity and unity: the same water in vapour, rain, river, tea, and us.
5. **It follows you home**  
   Everyone sees clouds. We hope the next one you see brings a little of this back.
6. **It is forgiving**  
   Dim, diffuse light feels primal and hides the imperfections of whatever room we are in.

### Section 2.4: Design decisions

**Eyebrow:** How we reasoned  
**Title:** Why it is the way it is  
**Lede:** Every element of the installation answers a question about belonging. These are the decisions we made, and our reasons for them.

1. **Breath as the interface**  
   *Reason:* Breath is both voluntary and involuntary, and everyone has it. Breathing in rhythm with something is one of the simplest forms of co-regulation. It also builds on what Zéphir learned from Réespiration.
2. **Breathing with the cloud, not controlling it**  
   *Reason:* We want people to feel that they belong with the cloud, not that they are in charge of it. Its light follows the rise and fall of your respiration, like a companion keeping time with you, rather than obeying commands.
3. **Lightning at dusk**  
   *Reason:* People arrive carrying their day. As the sun sets, the first lightning pulls attention into the room and towards weather: nature as vast, a little wild, and very present.
4. **A magical twilight before the breath**  
   *Reason:* The twilight turns the experience into a story. As its light intensifies and colours flare up through the whole cloud at once, the cloud gains a presence and a character. By the time it begins to breathe with you, a bond is already there.
5. **Lying down, shoes off**  
   *Reason:* Posture changes the role you play. Lying on the floor under a sky turns a visitor into an inhabitant, and the small ritual of preparation already begins the shared experience.
6. **No instructions about feelings**  
   *Reason:* Being told to feel connected is the fastest way not to be. The space offers conditions and a little mystery, and leaves the story to the people in it.

### Section 2.5: Open questions

**Eyebrow:** An open invitation  
**Title:** Questions we are living with  
**Lede:** We explored belonging to nature. Belonging to ourselves, to each other, to places and to communities are waiting to be explored too. We don’t have answers to these questions. If any of them pull at you, take them into your own practice, and tell us what you find.

#### Questions about belonging:
- Can we belong to weather? To a sky, a cloud, a season?
- How do you design for connection that is felt, not performed?
- What trace can a one-off encounter leave, so that people might meet again?
- When everyone breathes together, where does my breath end?

#### Questions about the cloud:
- How do we invite someone to breathe with the cloud without instructing them?
- How much of the twilight’s story should be told, and how much left to the imagination?
- How does the bond with the cloud carry on after you leave?
- Where could it live next: a gallery, a public space, a train carriage, a boat?
- What would a space for belonging to each other, or to ourselves, look like?
