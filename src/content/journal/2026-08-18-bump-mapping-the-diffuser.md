The [alpha](../2026-07-30-the-alpha-cloud/) showed that people would happily lie under a glowing cloud. It also showed where the experience felt flat. In August we went back to the studio to refine it, starting with the light.

## Just the skin

This time there was no fibre. We tested the cloud with only its nylon diffuser stretched over the mesh shell, to see what the surface does on its own before anything soft is layered on top.

## Faking depth with light

Bump mapping is a trick from computer graphics: shading a flat surface as if light were raking across bumps and hollows, so it reads as having relief. We tried the same idea on the physical cloud. Instead of lighting it evenly, we moved gradients across the LEDs so the light swept over the folds of the diffuser, lighting one side of a ridge and leaving the other in shadow.

Even on bare nylon, the moving light made the shape feel more sculpted and alive than a flat wash of colour. The creases and seams of the diffuser, which we had half hoped to hide, became part of the relief. It suggested the light could do more of the work of making the cloud feel three-dimensional, and that the fibre only needs to soften it, not create the form.
