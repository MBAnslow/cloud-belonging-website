Michael showed the first **lit cloud bottom**: a mass of fibre with LEDs inside it, glowing in the dark. Even this rough version changed the mood of the room. In the roadmap, the idea was to build just the underside of the cloud and hang it with its lights, to see how it looks and to test the breath sensors against it.

We ordered an **LED matrix**. Rather than a single colour wash, a grid of individually addressable lights could create gradients, travelling glows and flickers — maybe even the illusion that the cloud is slowly moving. It could give a sense of atmosphere, and of lightning.

Questions for this stage:

- How do we map light to the cloud's internal structure?
- Sun and moon as two separate sources, or a single light playing both?
- How do lightning and thunder line up, so a flash inside the cloud and a rumble in the room feel like one event?
