## Playing the cloud

An image for the sound design: each **out-breath becomes a sound object** that drifts through the space. When it reaches the cloud, it *plays* the cloud like an instrument. When several people's notes arrive together, they form chords. Held chords become drones that grow louder over time, signalling a **phase transition**. The ambient light follows, reflecting the transition and the intensity of the chords.

The shape of the experience also settled that day:

1. A long **night** of just breathing, with no augmentation at all.
2. A rising **crescendo** of playing the cloud together, which brings the dawn.
3. **Daytime**, and plain breath again.

## Being the sun

We tried lighting the tulle cloud with a **projector from below**. The beams through the tulle looked beautiful, and the cloud cast its shadow onto the ceiling.

That suggested an inversion. If the light comes from beneath, *we* are the sun, projecting onto the cloud from below, and the ceiling becomes the Earth. The shadows on the ceiling suddenly make sense. There could be a dramatic, disorienting reveal: people believe they are on the ground, then realise they are the sky.

Practical notes: it's hard to keep the cloud's structure legible from a single point of view. A web structure above might help, perhaps with **elastic lines**, so the cloud can deform and still keep its approximate shape.
