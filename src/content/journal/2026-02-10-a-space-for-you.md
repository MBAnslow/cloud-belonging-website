The project began as a proposal written by Zéphir for the Art & Cognition course at ENS-PSL, after a visit to Sony CSL Paris. Its question was simple and large: **to what extent can art and technology support the discovery of mindful practices — and put a little peace back into an increasingly chaotic world?**

## Where it came from

Zéphir had spent the previous year running a pilot study on *Réespiration*, an installation developed between EnsadLab and the AP-HP Pitié-Salpêtrière hospital. A chest-like object breathes with the visitor, whose breath is read by a thermal camera, while music and light follow the same rhythm.

The early accounts were striking in their **heterogeneity**. Some people described deep relaxation, even meditative or hypnotic-like states. Others found the same experience oppressive, or simply boring. For us, that spread of responses is the most useful thing the pilot taught: an immersive work doesn't produce a single effect. It meets whoever arrives.

## What the new proposal changed

Compared with *Réespiration*, *A Space For You* wanted to be:

- **public** — a place anyone could walk into, not a hidden hospital room;
- **light** — modular enough to travel between venues;
- **collective** — responsive to a group, not only to one person's breath;
- **primed** — exploring whether a welcome, a suggestion or a guided voice deepens the experience.

The first sketch was a room of cushions, soft lights and surround sound, in three movements: *explore*, *contemplate*, *land*. Participants' physiology would gently shape the light and music through [Funiki](https://csl.sony.fr/projects/funiki/), a system for composing dynamic atmospheres.

> A space to pause — for individual introspection, grounding and relaxation, but collectively, so participants can exchange on their experiences afterwards.

That last clause — *collectively* — is the seed that later grew into belonging.
