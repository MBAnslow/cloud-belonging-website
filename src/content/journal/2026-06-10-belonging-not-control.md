## Control vs. Belonging

A question that reshaped the project: **how do you make people feel they belong, rather than feel that they control?**

Belonging and control are very different things. There needs to be a sense of being part of something other than yourself to feel like you belong. This can infact involve letting go of control to some extent. 

This is important when considering interactive design and an important dimension to consider.

Being controlling is often a problematic personality trait, especially when that involves controlling others. It can also express itself as an attempt to force belonging by injecting yourself into the life of another person, but without the compromises and interpersonal balance that go with belonging.

## Playing a role

What would control in the context of belonging look like?

We considered the idea of **playing a role** as a way to circumvent the problems of control. You are not the role you play, and in some regards that makes the role bigger than you are. You act in that role and on behalf of that role and so the control you exert is measured against something larger than yourself. Roles exist within wider societal structures with conventions and laws woven into that structure. So in some sense, when playing a role, you are integrated in a network to which you belong. Though you have control, it is in service of something else and ideally in harmony with something else.

What would the roles of nature look like? How would you play that role? 

> It would be great if they could feel like a cloud for a while, and enter the logic of the cloud.

Perhaps the natural patterns in nature such as the day and night cycle act with rhythms that align with your breath? Perhaps for a moment your breath plays the role? Breath itself is interesting, because it is a largely involuntary and automatic act. Your breath connects something fundamental in you to the role you are performing.

What if, like in many mythological stories, an aspect of your physiology ensures the seasons are in harmony, the sun rises, the rain comes and waters the crops?

## Interbeing or belonging?

We held the two words side by side. **Interbeing**, Thich Nhat Hanh's term, never really goes away: a cloud doesn't die, it continues as rain, as river, as tea. It is a continued sense of being through time, and a connection through that. **Belonging** is warmer and more social, and more fragile. The project lives between the two.

## Water, night and day

- Could we play with the forms of water: vapour, liquid, ice?
- **Impermanence** cuts both ways. Hard things pass, and so do good ones, which is a reason for gratitude.
- A night that ends in daylight gives the experience its arc. *Daylight is the end of the experience.*
- Stars? We weren't sure yet.
