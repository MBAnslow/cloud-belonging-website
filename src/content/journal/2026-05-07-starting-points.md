The first weeks were deliberately wide. On the table were several possible works:

- a **biofeedback system** for collective meditation, built on Funiki;
- a **"state of mind visualiser"** based on dynamical systems — showing that anxious states are local minima we can be nudged out of, and that we are more than our current state;
- a **"you are the hero"** narrative, where a group gradually transforms an ambience through reflection and cooperation;
- a **giant cloud** about *interbeing* and *impermanence*;
- a mindful telephone box.

## Light as a metaphor for attention

The dynamical-systems idea left us with a vocabulary we kept: brightness as attention (how much you choose to see of something), saturation as optimism, and uncomfortable flicker as anxiety — something to reduce, or learn to cope with.

## Sensors and controllers

We surveyed what could read a room: heart-rate chest straps, EEG headbands, respiration belts, skin conductance wearables, phone accelerometers, microphones, pressure mats, cameras. We rated each for price, reliability in a live installation, and comfort. Breath kept winning. It is voluntary and involuntary at once, visible, and shared by everyone.

## Two routes to belonging

Our notes from that week sketch two ways a group might come to feel like a *we*:

1. **Shared perception** — we saw the same thing, we went through the same novel experience.
2. **Shared agency** — we influenced something together.

The second came with an image we kept returning to: *carrying a large weight with several others. At some point you're no longer sure whether you're doing the carrying.* Does that make you lose interest, or does it bring trust that you still contribute?
