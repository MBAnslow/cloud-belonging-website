That week we went to see how others hold an audience: *Le Grand Hôtel des Rêves*, and *TOI*, an immersive personality test by Moir. We paid close attention to thresholds, costumes, hosts, and the moment when a visitor stops being a visitor.

We also came back to psychology. Michael shared his writing on belonging, including on Maslow and the Blackfoot. Out of that came a simple realisation: **you cannot have this experience without the participants.** If the work is about how people bring belonging to each other, it is deeply relational. It can't just be streamed and understood.

## A conversation about ritual

A conversation between Zéphir and Joanna gave us a set of principles we still use.

**Make a parody of what not to do.** Imagine a voice booming *YOU FEEL GOOD. YOU FEEL CONNECTED.* If you tell people what to feel, they are more likely to leave. If you give them food for thought, they stay and contemplate.

**Start with a little ritual.** Take off your shoes. Maybe put on a robe — not to look like monks, but to be unified in something.

**Leave room for people to bring their own narrative.** Plant seeds of inquiry. Let the space ask questions, hold a small mystery, or suggest relationships people had not considered.

**Invite them to bring something.** An object that means something, and a chance to say what it means, in the spirit of Dharma sharing circles, where people listen without judgement or reply. Being heard does the work: *I hear you. I feel validated. I'm accepted.*

**Make the space intuitively non-threatening.** Then pick a few pillars and make sure they are unmistakably present.

Other ideas from the week: a mindful train carriage, a bus whose destination you don't know, a barge. And the *Regards connectés* exercise, where two people hold eye contact for a long time. After how long does the experience begin?
