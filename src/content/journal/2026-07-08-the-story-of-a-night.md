## Simulating before building

To think through these phases, we built a **3D simulation** of the room. Each participant is a figure lying on a mat, with their own cloud above them. Their breath is shown as a wave with its own rate, depth and phase. A *room synchrony* measure rises as the breaths align, and a lightning storm can be tuned for frequency and intensity. In the simulation, as synchrony reaches its peak, the individual clouds turn white and daylight rises.

The installation now has a story. **Belonging in ourselves, in others and in the environment are always connected, but this time the emphasis is on the environment.** A cloud is a thing above you, a thing that interacts with light, and something we connect with at different times of day.

## The phases

- **Before:** maybe a short reflection. Imagine a safe place, like a garden, to bring people into the space they carry with them.
- **I · Dusk:** enter at dusk or late evening. Sunset is like the lights going down in a cinema. Only ambient light; no light inside the cloud.
- **II · Lightning storm:** a point of potential disorientation before arriving at harmony. It pulls people out of whatever they were feeling before. Feeling exposed to the elements: nature as danger, the darker side of awe.
- **III · Moonlight:** it settles.
- **IV · Shared breath:** breath modulates the light. **Synchrony moves time forward towards sunrise.** When the group stays in step long enough, the system gradually takes the signal back, and their influence slowly fades.
- **V · Sunrise:** passive observation.
- **Reflection:** a tea ceremony.

Open questions: how do we invite people to breathe? Will they understand that they should breathe *together*? Do we frame it as a goal, like "save a cloud", or leave it unspoken?

## Synchronising breathing like fireflies

We looked at how fireflies synchronise their flashes as a possible inspiration for that shared breathing. On a [phase synchronisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_synchronization) reading, each flash is a point on a cycle, and neighbouring flies nudge one another until the cycles line up. We approximated that with simple sine waves.
